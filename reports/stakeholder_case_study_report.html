Business problem and prediction

ShopSpark currently fills recommendation slots with broad rules such as best sellers, recent views, and discounts. Those rules do not fully adapt to the user, product, placement, device, and moment.

Prediction task: for each user–product impression, estimate the probability that the displayed product will be clicked (clicked = 1). ShopSpark can use that probability to order eligible products from most to least likely to receive a click.

Business objective

Increase engagement with recommendation slots by prioritizing products with greater predicted click probability.

Model output

A probability between 0 and 1 for every impression—not simply a yes/no label.

Success criterion

Place real clicks near the top while maintaining credible probabilities on future, unseen data.

Analytical approach

  1. Understand and join. Validated keys, missingness, duplicates, time coverage, target balance, and joined impressions to user and product attributes.
  2. Clean and encode. Checked impossible ranges, standardized missing markers, compressed skew in price and review counts, imputed from training data, and encoded categories safely.
  3. Engineer predictive signals. Added time, price, discount, context interactions, cold-start indicators, and leakage-safe historical behavior.
  4. Establish baselines. Compared dummy, logistic regression, and a single decision tree to understand linearity, nonlinear value, and overfitting.
  5. Tune ensembles. Exhaustively evaluated eight parameter-grid configurations each for Gradient Boosting, AdaBoost, XGBoost, and Random Forest.
  6. Select, lock, and test. Chose the highest validation PR-AUC model, then evaluated only that winner on the test period.

Key engineered signals

Six derived numerical signals were screened using their absolute training correlation with the target. All historical target features were computed from earlier events only.

  • Product past CTR: smoothed historical click rate for the product.
  • User–product CTR gap: difference between user and product historical tendencies.
  • Product past clicks: previous clicks accumulated by the product.
  • Category past CTR: earlier click rate for the category.
  • Brand past CTR: earlier click rate for the brand.
  • Category–page CTR gap: contrast between category and page-type history.

Leakage control: training histories exclude the current click; validation and test histories use training aggregates only.

What stakeholders should take from feature importance

Feature importance identifies the inputs the winning model relied on most. It supports model review, leakage checks, and business explanation. It does not show causation or guarantee that changing a feature will change CTR.

  • Historical features must always be computed from information available before the impression.
  • High importance for prior product or category CTR means past engagement helps ranking; it does not mean the model should repeatedly show the same items without diversity controls.
  • Direction and individual explanations require SHAP or another local explanation method.

Limitations and safeguards

No true candidate sets

The data does not contain a recommendation request/session identifier plus every eligible product at that moment. Offline scores demonstrate prioritization, but cannot fully simulate within-request reranking.

Offline association, not causation

The test confirms predictive ranking, not incremental revenue or causal CTR lift. Only a controlled online experiment can measure that.

Feedback loops

Products shown more often gain more history. Exploration, minimum exposure, and diversity rules are needed to avoid reinforcing existing popularity.

Monitoring and fairness

Track calibration, CTR, coverage, cold-start performance, and segment-level quality over time. Review the use of demographic fields before production.

Recommended next steps

  1. Instrument candidate sets. Add request ID, session ID, all eligible products, current rule score, inventory, margin, and final displayed rank.
  2. Calibrate and constrain. Validate probability calibration and add inventory, diversity, freshness, safety, and commercial constraints.
  3. Shadow deployment. Score live traffic without changing rankings; verify latency, feature parity, and score stability.
  4. Run an A/B test. Compare XGBoost ranking with current rules using CTR as the primary outcome and conversion, revenue, margin, and user experience as guardrails.
  5. Monitor and retrain. Establish drift thresholds, segment dashboards, rollback criteria, and a retraining schedule.
